IPRoyal vs DataImpulse: Which Budget Proxy Provider Wins?

IPRoyal vs DataImpulse: two affordable pay-as-you-go proxy providers compared on pricing, proxy types, and features like non-expiring traffic.

Updated August 14, 2026

If you want reliable residential proxies without enterprise-level bills, IPRoyal and DataImpulse are two of the most popular ways to get them. Both are built on the same promise: no mandatory subscriptions, pay only for the traffic you use, and keep ethically sourced residential IPs affordable for indie developers, startups, and small teams. Because their pitches sound so similar, the choice usually comes down to details — traffic expiry, proxy-type lineup, targeting depth, and pricing philosophy. This comparison breaks down each of those in turn, so you can decide which budget provider actually fits the shape of your project.

Proxy Networks and Types

IPRoyal's network

IPRoyal's Royal Residential pool consists of millions of ethically sourced residential IPs with targeting down to country, state, and city level. Around that core it offers one of the broadest lineups in the budget segment: static residential (ISP) proxies for stable, trusted sessions; datacenter proxies for cheap, fast volume; mobile proxies for high-trust carrier IPs; and dedicated sneaker proxies — a niche product few budget competitors bother with. Sessions can be rotating or sticky, and SOCKS5 support gives you protocol flexibility beyond plain HTTP.

DataImpulse's network

DataImpulse also runs millions of ethically sourced residential IPs, spread across virtually every country, alongside mobile, datacenter, and ISP proxies — all managed from a single dashboard. Targeting covers country, region, and city, with sticky or rotating sessions. The integration story is deliberately plain: a standard endpoint and credentials that work with any scraper or HTTP client in any language. Where IPRoyal differentiates on lineup breadth, DataImpulse differentiates on keeping everything simple and in one place.

Who wins on the network?

Call it a narrow win for IPRoyal on versatility — sneaker proxies and SOCKS5 are real, if niche, advantages, and state-level targeting is a touch more specific than region-level. But for the core use cases both providers are chasing — rotating residential and mobile scraping on a budget — the networks are functionally comparable. If you're unsure which proxy type your project needs in the first place, our guide to proxy types explained walks through the trade-offs.

Standout Features

IPRoyal: traffic that never expires

IPRoyal's headline perk is non-expiring residential traffic. The GBs you buy sit in your account indefinitely — no monthly reset, no quiet expiry. For occasional or seasonal scrapers this is genuinely valuable: you can buy a chunk of traffic during a promotion, run your quarterly price-monitoring job, and still have the balance waiting six months later. It converts proxy bandwidth from a subscription-shaped cost into something closer to a stocked pantry.

DataImpulse: rock-bottom pricing, zero friction

DataImpulse is built around being one of the cheapest residential options on the market and making the buying process trivially simple. You top up a balance, pay per GB against it, and never touch a subscription. There is no clever perk to model — the pitch is simply that the same everyday scraping costs less here, and the unified dashboard means adding mobile, datacenter, or ISP proxies later doesn't mean learning a new product.

Pricing

Both providers use affordable pay-as-you-go pricing with no forced monthly commitment — that shared philosophy is why they end up head-to-head on so many shortlists. The differences are at the margins:

  • IPRoyal is competitively priced per GB, with the standout benefit that purchased residential traffic doesn't expire. Its effective cost advantage shows up over time: irregular users waste nothing.
  • DataImpulse is among the most affordable residential proxy providers, full stop. The top-up balance model makes it trivial to start small — fund the balance, scrape, refill when needed — and easy to scale spend up or down as projects come and go.

The practical guidance: if you scrape steadily and price-per-GB is your only metric, DataImpulse's positioning is hard to beat. If you scrape in bursts with quiet months in between, IPRoyal's non-expiring traffic may save you more than a lower sticker price would.

Promotional rates change often — check current per-GB pricing for IPRoyal and DataImpulse directly before you buy.

Performance

Both providers deliver reliable success rates on mainstream targets across their residential and mobile networks — standard e-commerce pages, content sites, and the broad middle of the web where most everyday scraping happens. Both also offer 24/7 live support, which is not a given in the budget segment.

The shared ceiling is at the top of the difficulty curve. Neither provider ships the advanced unblocking machinery — automated CAPTCHA solving, fingerprint management, dedicated unlocker products — that enterprise platforms use to crack the hardest anti-bot systems. If your targets are aggressively defended, read our guide on how to avoid getting blocked for the tactics involved, and consider a premium provider like Bright Data for that specific fight. For everything else, both of these networks offer excellent value.

Ease of Use

DataImpulse takes this category by a hair. Its single-dashboard design and balance-based billing make the whole experience feel like one product: top up, pick a proxy type, copy credentials, go. IPRoyal is also straightforward — standard credentials, familiar rotation and sticky-session options — but its wider catalog means slightly more surface area to navigate. Neither requires prior proxy experience, and both integrate with any scraper or HTTP client using standard configuration.

When to Choose IPRoyal

IPRoyal is the better fit if:

  • You scrape occasionally or seasonally. Non-expiring residential traffic means GBs bought today are still there whenever the next job lands — no waste, ever.
  • You want the widest budget lineup. Residential, ISP, datacenter, mobile, and sneaker proxies from a single account cover almost any everyday use case.
  • You need sneaker proxies. IPRoyal serves this niche directly; DataImpulse doesn't.
  • Your tooling needs SOCKS5. Protocol flexibility beyond plain HTTP.
  • You value fine geo-targeting. Country, state, and city-level targeting supports localized scraping tasks.

When to Choose DataImpulse

DataImpulse is the better fit if:

  • Absolute lowest cost is the priority. Its residential proxies are among the most affordable available, and that's the entire design goal.
  • You want the simplest possible model. Top up a balance, pay per GB, refill when needed — nothing else to think about.
  • You scrape steadily. Continuous usage doesn't benefit from banked traffic, so the cheaper per-GB positioning wins outright.
  • You want everything in one dashboard. Residential, mobile, datacenter, and ISP proxies managed from a single place.
  • You're an indie dev or early startup. The cost structure is built precisely for small, growing projects.

Verdict: IPRoyal vs DataImpulse

We rate IPRoyal 4.2/5 and DataImpulse 4.0/5 — a close call between two providers executing the same budget-friendly philosophy with different emphases. IPRoyal edges ahead on features: the non-expiring traffic is the single most useful perk in the budget proxy market, and the broader lineup (sneaker proxies, SOCKS5, state-level targeting) gives it more answers as your needs evolve. That makes it our default recommendation between the two.

DataImpulse remains the sharper pick when price is the whole question. If you scrape regularly, want the lowest realistic bill, and prefer one simple dashboard over a catalog of products, it delivers exactly that. And because both are pay-as-you-go with tiny commitments, running a small trial of each against your actual target sites is cheap — let your own success rates and effective cost per successful request decide.

FAQ

Which is cheaper, IPRoyal or DataImpulse?

DataImpulse generally positions itself as the lower-cost option — its residential proxies are among the most affordable on the market, billed against a simple top-up balance. IPRoyal is also competitively priced per GB, and its non-expiring residential traffic can make it cheaper in practice for occasional users, since nothing you buy goes to waste. Check current per-GB rates on both sites, as promotions change often.

Does DataImpulse traffic expire like a monthly plan?

DataImpulse uses a top-up balance model rather than a forced monthly subscription, so you buy traffic as you need it with no recurring commitment. IPRoyal goes a step further for its residential product: purchased GBs explicitly never expire, which is its headline perk for seasonal and occasional scrapers.

Do IPRoyal and DataImpulse offer the same proxy types?

Mostly. Both cover residential, mobile, datacenter, and ISP proxies. IPRoyal adds dedicated sneaker proxies and SOCKS5 support, while DataImpulse emphasizes serving all its proxy types from a single unified dashboard. For the common residential and mobile use cases, either provider has you covered.

Are IPRoyal or DataImpulse good enough for heavily protected sites?

They're built for value on mainstream targets, not for the hardest anti-bot systems. Both deliver reliable success rates on everyday sites, but neither includes the advanced unblocking tooling of a premium platform. For aggressively defended targets, a provider like Bright Data still has the edge.
